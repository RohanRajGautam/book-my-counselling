Information is everywhere. Guidance is rare.

## The problem with too much information

Students today have more data at their fingertips than any generation before. And yet the decisions feel harder, not easier. The signal-to-noise ratio has flipped — there's so much to read that the act of deciding gets pushed further and further down the calendar.

## What a good mentor does

A good mentor doesn't have more information than you. They have three things you can't get from a search engine:

- **Pattern recognition** — they've seen your situation before and can name what you're going through.
- **Honest feedback** — they'll tell you the part you don't want to hear.
- **Accountability** — someone is checking your work and asking what you shipped this week.

That's the part that matters. Not the content, but the conversation around it.

## How to find one

Start with someone two or three steps ahead of where you want to be. Reach out, be specific about what you're asking for, and follow through on the small things — that's how trust gets built.

Mentorship isn't magic. It's a practice. The earlier you start, the more compounding you get.
